The right back-office software connects the daily work of a small business: clients, quotes, invoices, expenses, documents, books and employee records. Whether you work in Switzerland, the EU or the US, check the currencies, document formats and local requirements you actually need. One shared workspace reduces duplicate data entry; specialist tools still make sense for complex needs.

This guide explains what to look for and when separate tools still make sense. For product-by-product details, see [bexio alternatives in Switzerland](/guides/bexio-alternatives).

## What is back-office software?

Back-office software is the set of tools a business uses for the admin around its work: clients, quotes and invoices, getting paid, expenses, documents, contracts and taxes. For a small Swiss business it also has to produce QR bills, follow up overdue invoices and prepare the VAT return.

The parts most small businesses need:

| Area             | What it covers                                                          |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Clients (CRM)    | Client records, contact persons, files and history                      |
| Quotes, invoices | Numbered documents, QR bill, credit notes, partial and deposit invoices |
| Getting paid     | Payments, reminders with fees and interest, instalment plans            |
| Expenses         | Receipts, input VAT, what is paid and what is still open                |
| Documents        | Contracts, signed PDFs, files shared with clients                       |
| Signatures       | Sending a contract or quote to sign electronically                      |
| VAT and books    | The VAT return and the figures your fiduciary needs                     |

## Is an all-in-one platform better than separate tools?

For most freelancers and small teams, yes: one platform means each client is entered once, invoices know about contracts and expenses, and there is one export for your fiduciary. Separate tools are better when one area is complex enough to need a specialist, such as payroll for many employees or a shop with a till.

Signs that separate tools cost you time:

- You type the same client into your CRM, your invoicing tool and your signature tool.
- You check your bank by hand to see which invoices are paid, then send reminders by hand.
- Signed contracts sit in an email inbox instead of next to the client.
- Your fiduciary gets a folder of PDFs at the end of the year and asks questions about each one.

An all-in-one platform also has a cost: you depend on one vendor, and each part may be simpler than a specialist tool. Check that you can export all your data, at any time, in formats other software reads.

## What does a solo consultant need, and what does a small team need?

A solo consultant needs fast quotes and invoices, a QR bill, reminders that send themselves, expenses with receipts and the VAT return once registered. A small team additionally needs shared records, roles that limit who can do what, and a clear record of who changed what.

| Need                                | Solo consultant     | Small team                        |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Quotes and invoices with QR bill    | Yes                 | Yes                               |
| Automatic reminders                 | Saves the most time | Yes                               |
| Expenses with receipts              | Yes                 | Yes, with who paid and reimbursed |
| Contracts to sign                   | Often               | Often                             |
| Roles and permissions               | No                  | Yes                               |
| Working in the same records at once | No                  | Yes                               |
| Audit log and closed periods        | Nice to have        | Important                         |
| Client portal                       | Rarely              | Often, with many clients          |

Look at the pricing model too. Some tools charge one price however many people use it (Magic Heidi), some include a number of users per tier (bexio, AbaNinja), and some charge per person (Helvetly, where every team member is a paid seat). A per-person price is simple for one or two people; a flat price can be cheaper for a larger team.

## What changes when you have clients abroad?

Clients abroad add three things to check: the currency (the QR bill accepts only CHF and EUR), Swiss VAT (services to clients abroad are generally invoiced without it) and the invoice format (many EU businesses ask for e-invoices). Pick software that lets you set the currency and language per client and invoice.

See [invoicing clients abroad and in foreign currencies](/guides/multi-currency-invoicing) for the details, and [e-invoicing for Swiss companies](/guides/e-invoicing-for-swiss-companies) for XRechnung, Peppol, KSeF and e-Factura. Helvetly exports these e-invoice files on the Business plan; delivery through the networks needs an outside provider.

## Can AI help with business administration?

Yes, within limits. AI can answer questions about your own records, read receipts and files into drafts, and prepare routine steps, but you should still confirm anything that sends, books or pays, and check figures against the source.

What Swiss tools offer, according to their own sites: bexio's AI reads receipts and QR invoices; KLARA has AI receipt scanning and connects an outside ChatGPT or Claude; Magic Heidi connects your own Claude or ChatGPT through MCP; AbaNinja's assistant SamurAI acts after confirmation.

Helvetly has Heidi, an assistant built in (Pro and Business), and AI import:

- **Heidi answers from your records:** overdue and recent invoices, this month's expenses and input VAT, recent clients and documents, engagement balances. She can open records, start a new invoice and log work on an engagement.
- **She asks first:** before Heidi logs work or sends an invoice, you confirm. She cannot issue invoices, book expenses or change settings on her own.
- **She can be wrong:** check figures against the source. Questions she cannot answer from your records go to an AI model (OpenAI), and only after you approve AI use in that workspace.
- **Some data stays out:** Helvetly does not fetch employee or payroll records for AI requests.
- **AI import** reads spreadsheets, PDFs, photos and Word files into clients, products, engagements, invoices and expenses, and shows them to you before anything is saved.

AI use counts against a monthly AI allowance. See [use Heidi](/help/use-heidi) and [import records with AI](/help/import-records-with-ai).

## A checklist for choosing back-office software

For a business operating in Switzerland, check that the software:

1. Creates Swiss QR bills with a QR reference or Creditor Reference and structured addresses ([the Swiss QR bill](/guides/qr-bill)).
2. Shows everything a Swiss VAT invoice needs ([what a Swiss invoice must contain](/guides/invoice-requirements)).
3. Sends reminders automatically, with fees and default interest if you charge them ([payment reminders](/guides/payment-reminders)).
4. Keeps expenses with their receipts and input VAT.
5. Prepares the VAT return with your method, ideally as the eCH-0217 file the ESTV ePortal imports ([Swiss VAT, explained](/guides/swiss-vat)).
6. Stores contracts and signed documents next to the client.
7. Handles your clients abroad: currencies, languages and, if asked, e-invoices.
8. Gives each team member the right access, and records who changed what.
9. Exports all your data, and gives your fiduciary what they need.
10. Has a price that still works with one more person or ten times the invoices.

Try the two or three best candidates with a real client, a real quote and a real invoice before moving everything. Helvetly's plans are on the [pricing page](/pricing).
