Most late payments are just forgotten payments. A clear, friendly reminder sent on time solves the majority of them. For the rest, Swiss law gives you a defined path: default, interest, and finally debt collection through the Betreibungsamt. This guide covers that path step by step.

## When is a payment legally late?

An invoice being past its due date does not automatically put your customer in default. Under Article 102 of the Swiss Code of Obligations (OR), there are two cases:

| Situation                                    | When default starts                                       |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| No fixed payment date was agreed             | When you send a reminder (Mahnung) after the claim is due |
| A fixed payment date was agreed (Verfalltag) | Automatically on that date, with no reminder needed       |

A fixed date means something both parties agreed in advance, for example "payment by 31 March" in the contract. A due date that only appears on your invoice is weaker evidence. If you are unsure whether a date was really agreed, send a reminder anyway. It costs nothing and removes any doubt about when default began.

The reminder does not need special wording. It must clearly ask the customer to pay. Keep a copy and note when and how you sent it.

## Default interest: 5% per year

Once the customer is in default, you may charge default interest (Verzugszins) under Article 104 OR:

- The statutory rate is **5% per year**.
- A higher rate applies if the contract says so.
- Between businesses, a higher rate also applies if the usual bank discount rate at the place of payment is above 5%.
- Interest on default interest is not allowed (Article 105 OR).

Interest runs from the day default starts until the day of payment. You do not need to prove that the delay cost you anything.

**Example:** CHF 5,000 paid 30 days late. 5,000 × 5% × 30 / 365 = **CHF 20.55**.

On small invoices the amount is modest. Many businesses still state it on the reminder, because it shows the delay has a cost.

## Reminder fees: only if agreed

There is no general legal right to a reminder fee (Mahngebühr) in Switzerland. You can charge one when the customer agreed to it in advance, typically in your contract or in general terms and conditions that were validly part of the contract.

Practical rules:

- Put the fee and the amount in your terms before the work starts, not on the first reminder.
- Keep fees proportionate to the actual effort. A fee that looks like a penalty invites disputes.
- Without an agreement, you can only claim further losses as damages under Article 106 OR, and you must prove them.

Debt collection costs are a separate matter: the creditor advances them, and they are charged to the debtor (Article 68 of the Debt Enforcement and Bankruptcy Act, SchKG).

## A typical reminder sequence

Swiss law does not prescribe the number of reminders. Three steps are common, but the right number depends on your customers and amounts.

| Step           | Timing (example)                     | Tone and content                                                    |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Friendly note  | A few days before or on the due date | Short heads-up with the invoice attached. Not a formal reminder.    |
| 1st reminder   | 7–10 days after the due date         | Polite. Assume an oversight. New payment deadline, usually 10 days. |
| 2nd reminder   | About 14 days later                  | Firmer. Mention default interest and any agreed fee.                |
| Final reminder | About 14 days later                  | Name the consequence: debt collection after a final date.           |

A few tips:

- **Always give a concrete new deadline** ("by 25 November"), never "as soon as possible".
- **Attach the original invoice and a payment slip** for the outstanding total, so paying takes one scan.
- **Show the calculation**: original amount, payments received, interest, agreed fee, total due.
- **Stop the sequence immediately** when a payment arrives or the customer disputes the invoice. A reminder sent after payment damages the relationship.
- **Call before the final reminder** on larger amounts. A phone call often uncovers the real reason: a missing PO number, a wrong address, a cash-flow problem.

## Wording that works

Clear, neutral language gets paid faster than threats. Some patterns:

- First reminder: "Our records show that invoice 2026-118 of 3 October (CHF 1,240.00) is still open. Perhaps it was overlooked. Please pay by 25 November. If you have already paid, please ignore this message."
- Second reminder: "Despite our reminder of 14 November, invoice 2026-118 remains unpaid. As agreed in our terms, we charge a reminder fee of CHF 20.00. Default interest of 5% per year applies from 14 November. Please pay the total shown below by 9 December."
- Final reminder: "If we do not receive the total shown below by 23 December, we will start debt collection proceedings without further notice."

Avoid vague threats, all capitals, and anything you are not prepared to do. If you write "final reminder", act on it.

## When to start debt collection (Betreibung)

If reminders fail, the next step is debt enforcement under the SchKG. You do not need a court judgment to start. A high-level overview:

1. **Request (Betreibungsbegehren).** You file it with the debt collection office (Betreibungsamt) at the debtor's domicile or registered office, and advance the fees.
2. **Payment order (Zahlungsbefehl).** The office serves it on the debtor, who has 20 days to pay.
3. **Objection (Rechtsvorschlag).** The debtor can object within 10 days, without giving reasons. This stops the procedure.
4. **Removing the objection.** With a court judgment you request definitive removal (Article 80 SchKG). With a debt acknowledgement signed by the debtor, you can request provisional removal (Article 82 SchKG). Otherwise you have to sue in ordinary proceedings.
5. **Continuation (Fortsetzungsbegehren).** If no valid objection stands, you can request continuation 20 days after service at the earliest, and within one year (Article 88 SchKG). Depending on the debtor, this leads to seizure (Pfändung) or, for most entities registered in the commercial register, bankruptcy proceedings (Article 39 SchKG).

When to take this step is a business decision. Starting is usually worth it when the amount justifies the effort, the claim is undisputed, and the customer has stopped responding. A debt collection entry also stops the limitation period running out: most claims become time-barred after 10 years (Article 127 OR), some, such as craftwork, retail sales and professional services, after 5 years (Article 128 OR), and debt collection interrupts that period (Article 135 OR).

If the customer disputes the invoice on the merits, talk to them or a lawyer first. Debt collection does not decide who is right; an objection sends you to court anyway.

## Checklist

- Payment terms, default interest rate and reminder fees written into your contract or terms.
- Reminder sent promptly after the due date, with a new concrete deadline.
- Interest calculated from the start of default at 5% per year, unless a higher rate was agreed.
- Fees only where agreed.
- Copies of every reminder kept.
- Debt collection started once a final deadline has passed without payment or reply.
