Swiss value added tax (VAT, in German MWST, in French TVA) is a federal tax on almost every sale of goods and services in Switzerland. Registered businesses add it to their prices, deduct the VAT they paid on their own purchases, and pay the difference to the Federal Tax Administration (ESTV). This guide covers the rules a small business needs day to day.

## Who must register

You are liable for VAT once your business reaches **CHF 100,000 a year in turnover** from supplies that are not exempt from the tax. The figure counts your turnover in Switzerland **and abroad**, so export sales and services to foreign clients count too.

| Situation                                                          | Threshold   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------- |
| Most businesses, including sole proprietors                        | CHF 100,000 |
| Volunteer-run, non-profit sports and cultural clubs, and charities | CHF 250,000 |

Legal form does not matter: a sole proprietor, a GmbH and an AG face the same threshold.

When does it start?

- **New business:** if you expect to exceed the threshold within the first 12 months, you are liable from the day you start.
- **Existing business:** if you did not expect it but your turnover in the last business year reached the threshold, the exemption ends and you must register.
- **Registration deadline:** within **30 days** of becoming liable. You register online with the ESTV and receive a VAT number in the form CHE-123.456.789 MWST.

Below the threshold you may register voluntarily. See [VAT for the self-employed](/guides/vat-for-the-self-employed) for when that makes sense.

## Rates

| Rate                      | Applies to                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **8.1 %** (standard)      | Everything not listed below: most services, consulting, software, restaurant meals, alcohol                         |
| **2.6 %** (reduced)       | Food and non-alcoholic drinks (not in restaurants), water, medicines, books, newspapers, menstrual hygiene products |
| **3.8 %** (accommodation) | Overnight stays including breakfast in hotels and holiday rentals                                                   |

These rates apply since 1 January 2024. Some supplies, such as most medical care, education, insurance and the rental of housing, are **exempt**: no VAT is charged, but no input tax can be deducted on the costs either.

A change is on the way to the ballot: on **29 November 2026** voters decide on raising VAT to finance the 13th AHV pension. If accepted, the standard rate would rise to 8.5 % and the accommodation rate to 4.0 % from 2028; the reduced rate would stay at 2.6 %. Until then, the rates above apply.

## Effective method or net tax rate method

There are two ways to work out what you owe.

**Effective method (the default).** You charge VAT on each sale, add up the VAT you paid on business purchases (input tax) and pay the difference. It is exact, and it suits businesses with large purchases or investments, or with many exports.

**Net tax rate method (Saldosteuersatz).** You still charge your clients the normal rate, for example 8.1 %. But you pay the ESTV a fixed percentage of your gross turnover (VAT included) and claim no input tax. The ESTV sets the percentage per industry so that it reflects typical costs. There are ten rates, from 0.1 % to 6.8 %; consulting, IT services, architecture and fiduciary offices, for example, have 6.2 %.

|                    | Effective method            | Net tax rate method                                               |
| ------------------ | --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Who can use it     | Everyone                    | Up to CHF 5,024,000 turnover **and** up to CHF 108,000 tax a year |
| Input tax          | Deducted invoice by invoice | Not deducted, already built into the rate                         |
| Returns per year   | 4 (quarterly)               | 2 (half-yearly)                                                   |
| Bookkeeping effort | Higher                      | Lower                                                             |
| Several activities | One calculation             | A separate rate for each activity above 10 % of turnover          |

Both limits count. At a 6.2 % rate, the CHF 108,000 tax limit is reached at about CHF 1.74 million of turnover, long before the turnover limit.

**Choosing and switching.** A newly registered business must apply for the net tax rate method within **60 days** of receiving its VAT number. Later, a switch takes effect at the start of a tax period and must be reported within the first 60 days of that period. You must keep the net tax rate method for at least one full tax period; after choosing the effective method, you can only move to the net tax rate method after three years. On a switch, input tax already claimed on assets still in use is partly paid back or recovered, so ask before switching with large investments on the books.

## Reporting periods and deadlines

| Situation                                | Return                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Effective method                         | Quarterly                                                                                                    |
| Net tax rate method                      | Half-yearly                                                                                                  |
| On request, turnover up to CHF 5,005,000 | Yearly, with instalments set by the ESTV (quarterly for the effective method, half-yearly for net tax rates) |
| On request, regular input tax surplus    | Monthly                                                                                                      |

The return **and the payment** are due **60 days after the end of the period**. For a quarter ending 31 March, that is 30 May. The tax period is the calendar year.

Yearly returns have been possible since 2025. You must apply within 60 days of the start of the year (or of receiving your VAT number) and keep yearly reporting for at least one full tax period.

## Agreed or received basis

This decides **when** VAT becomes due.

- **Agreed basis (vereinbarte Entgelte, the default):** VAT on a sale is due in the period in which you issue the invoice, whether or not the client has paid. Input tax may be deducted when you receive a supplier's invoice.
- **Received basis (vereinnahmte Entgelte, on request):** VAT is due only when the client pays, and input tax is deducted when you pay.

The received basis helps your cash flow if clients pay late, but you must track payments rather than invoices. It must be kept for at least one tax period. The ESTV can require it if you are often paid before you deliver.

## Filing in the ESTV ePortal

Since 1 January 2025, VAT returns can only be filed online in the **ESTV ePortal**; paper forms no longer exist. You sign in with AGOV, the federal login, and either fill in the form field by field (each field is a numbered _Ziffer_) or upload an XML file in the **eCH-0217** standard (version 2.0.0) that your software creates. Uploading avoids retyping figures.

After filing, you pay the amount due with the payment details the ESTV gives you. If you file late, the ESTV sends a reminder and can estimate your tax; late payments cost default interest.

## Corrections

Errors happen, and the law expects you to fix them.

- **When closing your books:** errors you find while preparing your annual accounts must be corrected at the latest in the return for the period that includes the **180th day after your business year ends**. For a calendar year, that is the return for the second quarter (or first half) of the next year. In practice this is the yearly reconciliation (_Finalisierung_): you compare your accounts with the returns filed and submit a correction return in the ePortal.
- **Older errors:** you must also correct errors you discover in earlier years, as long as those years are not final. The right to assess VAT normally lapses **five years** after the end of the tax period, and in every case after **ten years**.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting only Swiss turnover** for the threshold. Turnover abroad counts too.
- **Registering late.** The 30-day deadline runs from the day you became liable, and the ESTV can register you retroactively, so you owe VAT you never charged.
- **Showing VAT on invoices without being registered.** Anyone who shows VAT on an invoice owes it, even when not registered.
- **Using the wrong net tax rate** or ignoring a second activity that has grown above 10 % of turnover.
- **Forgetting acquisition tax.** Services bought from foreign suppliers who are not registered in Switzerland, such as software subscriptions, can make you owe VAT on them yourself (acquisition tax). Businesses not otherwise registered owe it once such purchases exceed CHF 10,000 a year.
- **Deducting input tax from invoices without a supplier VAT number**, or from private expenses.
- **Skipping the yearly reconciliation** and discovering differences only at a tax audit.
